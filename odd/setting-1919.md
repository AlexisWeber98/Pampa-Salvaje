# Setting guide: La Pampa, 1919

The game opens in **1919**. Every character, building and prop must fit that year, including the people we add later. This is a design reference for clothes and atmosphere, not a history book. Where real details are uncertain, choose what reads clearly as "late 1910s rural Argentina" over strict accuracy.

## Place and time

- The towns (General Pico, Monte Nievas, Eduardo Castex) are young railway towns, founded in the first decade of the 1900s around the stations of the British-owned railways that crossed the pampa.
- La Pampa was then a **National Territory** (Territorio Nacional de La Pampa Central), not a province. Its police was the territorial police, appointed from Buenos Aires.
- Life ran on horses, carts (sulkys, chatas) and the train. Cars were rare in small towns. **Do not add cars or trucks.**
- Streets were dirt. Light came from kerosene lamps and the station's lamps. There was no electric street lighting in small towns.
- The general store (almacén de ramos generales) sold everything, and it was often also the bar and post office. The station, the church, the comisaría, the plaza and the store made up the civic core.

## Clothes, by role

### The protagonist (user, 2026-10-21)
He was a hardware-shop clerk in General Pico, a small provincial town, and he is **not wealthy**. Most people in this world are humble townsfolk, and he is one of them.
- A plain work shirt (collarless or with a soft collar) and a **leather jacket**, worn and a little scuffed.
- **Worn trousers**, faded wool or drill.
- **Boots**, plain and used.
- A **wide-brimmed soft felt hat, a "gacho"**, with the brim slightly turned down. It looks like a wide fedora.
- No suit, no stiff collar, no tie, no bowler. His clothes are townsman's working clothes, not the gaucho's, which marks him as someone from town rather than the countryside.

### Town gentlemen (later: the doctor, the station master on Sunday, a notary)
- Only the few well-off people wear a three-piece suit, a high stiff collar and tie, and a bowler (bombín) or straw boater (rancho), with laced shoes.
- These are the exception in a small town, not the norm.

### Townsfolk and peones (rural workers)
- **Bombacha** (loose trousers gathered at the ankle), a **faja** (sash) or a wide leather belt (**rastra** with coins for the better-off), a collarless shirt, and a vest.
- **Alpargatas** (rope-soled canvas shoes) or worn boots.
- **Boina** (beret, common among Basque, Spanish and Italian immigrants) or a soft wide-brimmed hat.
- A **pañuelo** knotted at the neck. A **poncho** in cold weather.
- Many townsfolk were European immigrants (Spanish, Italian, Basque, Russian-German "alemanes del Volga"), so clothing mixed European and criollo styles.

### Estancieros (Don Hidalgo)
- A wide-brimmed felt hat, a good jacket or a riding coat, a waistcoat, bombacha or riding trousers, tall leather boots, and a rastra.
- An older man, often with a white moustache. Quality cloth: clean and not ragged.

### Police (comisario Elías Farach)
- A **kepi** (cap with a visor), a dark blue or black **tunic with brass buttons**, dark trousers and boots.
- A leather belt, often with a sabre for officers.
- A comisario may wear a more formal or civilian-mixed outfit than the agents. Keep him readable as police: kepi plus buttoned tunic.

### Railway employees (to be added later)
- A **peaked cap** with the company badge, and a dark uniform jacket with buttons and a waistcoat.
- The station master looks more formal. The guard carries a lantern and flags. Labourers on the line wear work clothes and a cap.

### Women
- **Long skirts to the ankle**, a long-sleeved blouse with a high neck, and an apron for work.
- A **shawl or mantilla**, and hair worn up. A headscarf (pañuelo) for rural women, a hat for town ladies.
- No short skirts and no bare arms.

### Children (later)
- Smaller versions of the adult clothes: short trousers with long socks for boys, and dresses below the knee for girls.

## Social reality

Most characters are **humble**: clerks, peones, farmhands, railway workers and shop owners. Their clothes are mended, faded and dusty. Fine clothes are the exception and mark status: the estanciero, the comisario's uniform, the odd town gentleman.

## Palette

- Earthy and muted: dust, ochre, faded blue, brown, grey and black wool, and off-white shirts.
- Bright colours appear only as accents: the red or white pañuelo, a coloured faja, the brass police buttons.

## Buildings and props

- Brick or adobe houses with flat roofs and parapets, or corrugated-iron roofs.
- Water tanks, windmills (molinos) and grain sheds near the station.
- Hitching posts, carts, barrels, sacks of grain, and kerosene lamps.
- Wire fences (alambrados) and gates (tranqueras) between fields. Caldén and chañar trees.

## Rule

Any new character, outfit, building or prop must be checked against this guide before it is built. If it clashes with 1919, change it or ask the user.
